By day I build AI systems: agents that plan, remember, and argue with each other until they get it right. By night I'm somewhere between a neuroscience paper, a Dostoevsky novel and a beat that isn't finished yet.

It looks like four hobbies. It's one question.

<h3 align="center"><i>How does anything turn patterns into meaning?</i></h3>

A brain does it with neurons. A model does it with weights. A novel does it with foreshadowing. A song does it with tension and release. I go wherever that question shows up.

## A few things that are true

- I made two neural networks **argue** over skin-lesion images, and a third model **settle the argument**. &nbsp;→&nbsp; [ArgusVision](https://github.com/Ahmadhassan30/ArgusVision)
- I built a **C compiler framework in Rust**, designed so every transformation is traceable. &nbsp;→&nbsp; [Aether](https://github.com/Ahmadhassan30/Aether)
- I turn **lectures into animated explainers** without a single video-generation model. &nbsp;→&nbsp; [Anemo](https://github.com/Ahmadhassan30/Anemo)
- You can **sketch a website on paper**, snap a photo, and get working Next.js back. &nbsp;→&nbsp; [Morpheus](https://github.com/Ahmadhassan30/Morpheus)
- I **produce music**, and I built my own BPM, key and scale detector. &nbsp;→&nbsp; [Harmony](https://github.com/Ahmadhassan30/Harmony)
- I read Dostoevsky **as AI research**. The shelf below is not decoration.

## What I'm working on

<img align="right" width="230" src="assets/avatar.gif" alt="Ahmad at his laptop, thinking, then getting an idea">

**Computational neuroscience.** Computational models of the brain, and what they can teach machines.

**Resilient multi-agent reasoning.** Agents that coordinate under uncertainty, keep memory across long contexts, and change course when they're wrong.

**Emergent alignment.** The quiet preferences and biases instruction-tuned models pick up in training, the ones benchmarks miss.

**Retrieval past naive RAG.** Knowledge graphs fused with dense vectors and tool outputs, so a system can reason about what it retrieves.

## The shelf

<a href="https://ahmadhassan30.github.io/Ahmadhassan30/shelf/"><img src="assets/shelf.webp" width="100%" alt="A 3D bookshelf in three.js. A cursor hovers Dostoevsky, Kafka, Nietzsche and Plath, each book tipping out with a note; Tolstoy flies forward and opens to a quote."></a>

Literature is compressed causal reasoning: plots that pay off three hundred pages later, narrators you can't trust, foreshadowing that works as prediction. That's exactly what reasoning systems struggle with, so I read it as research.

**[Open the shelf ↗](https://ahmadhassan30.github.io/Ahmadhassan30/shelf/)** It's a live three.js scene: hover a book to pull it out, click to open it. Or **[recommend me one](https://github.com/Ahmadhassan30/Ahmadhassan30/issues/new?title=Book%20recommendation%3A%20&body=Title%3A%0AAuthor%3A%0AWhy%20I%27d%20like%20it%3A%0A)**.

## In the studio

<img align="left" width="220" src="assets/studio.gif" alt="Ahmad in headphones at a pad controller, nodding along to a beat">

I make music. Producing is pattern work too: build tension, hold it, release it at exactly the right moment, and trust the listener to wait for the payoff.

It's also why [Harmony](https://github.com/Ahmadhassan30/Harmony) exists. Drop in any track and it tells you the BPM, key and scale, with several detection algorithms voting on the answer.
